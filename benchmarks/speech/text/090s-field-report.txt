We reached the coastal monitoring station shortly after sunrise. The road was clear, but a strong wind had scattered branches across the path from the car park. Before unloading the equipment, we checked the notice board and called the station manager to confirm that the lower trail was open. Our first task was to replace the battery in the weather sensor above the north entrance. The old battery still held a charge, so we labelled it for testing rather than throwing it away.

After that, we walked down to the observation platform and photographed the water level against the marked posts. A fishing boat passed just as the second measurement began, and its wake made the reading unreliable. We waited for the surface to settle and repeated the measurement. Both sets of notes will remain in the record, with an explanation of why the first reading was rejected.

By midmorning, clouds were gathering beyond the headland. We covered the spare instruments, checked that the storage cabinet was locked, and moved the remaining work indoors. The station manager asked us to leave a simple checklist for the next team. I suggested putting the emergency contact details at the top, followed by the equipment checks and the location of the spare keys. We finished the visit by comparing our notes and confirming that every photograph had a matching entry in the field log.
